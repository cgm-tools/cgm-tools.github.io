    Method

    For a single absorber with a Maxwellian velocity distribution of Doppler parameter b, the optical depth at dimensionless frequency offset $u = \Delta v / b$ is $\tau(u) = \tau_0\,H(a,u)$, where $H$ is the Voigt function, normalised to $H(a,0)\to 1$ as $a\to0$:

    $$\tau_0 = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,e^2}{m_e c}\,\frac{f\lambda_0 N}{b} = 1.4974\times10^{-15}\, \frac{f\,(\lambda_0/\text{Å})\,(N/\text{cm}^{-2})}{b/\text{km s}^{-1}}, \qquad a = \frac{\Gamma \lambda_0}{4\pi b}.$$

    The normalized flux profile shown in Figure 2 is $F(\Delta v) = \exp[-\tau_0\,H(a,\Delta v/b)]$, and the rest-frame equivalent width is its absorbed area,

    $$W_r = \frac{\lambda_0}{c}\int\left[1-F(\Delta v)\right]d\Delta v = \frac{\lambda_0 b}{c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[1-e^{-\tau_0 H(a,u)}\right]du ,$$

    which has the familiar limits $W_r = (\pi e^2/m_e c^2)\,N f \lambda_0^2$ on the linear part ($\tau_0 \ll 1$), $W_r \simeq 2(\lambda_0 b/c)\sqrt{\ln\tau_0}$ on the flat part, and $W_r \simeq 2(\lambda_0 b/c)\,(\sqrt{\pi}\,\tau_0 a)^{1/2}$ on the damping part ($\tau_0 a \gg 1$). $H(a,u)$ is evaluated with the Humlíček (1982) W4 algorithm. The integral covers the full profile: Simpson's rule through the Doppler core, a logarithmic grid through the Lorentzian wings, and the analytic wing contribution beyond the last grid point. Against an independent calculation (scipy.special.wofz with adaptive quadrature) the curves agree to better than 10−4 for log N = 10–22 and b = 5–100 km s−1, and the profiles of Figure 2 integrate to the same Wr to better than 10−4 (test page).

    Wavelengths, oscillator strengths and damping constants are read from Morton (2003) for λ > 912 Å and from Verner et al. (1996) for EUV and X-ray lines. Lines without a tabulated Γ use the sum of the radiative rates out of the same upper level in the same compilation, which is a lower limit and only affects the damping part.
